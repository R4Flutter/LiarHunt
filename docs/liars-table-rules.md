# Liar's Table: paper test

The goal is to find out whether Liar's Table is fun before building it. Play both versions with 4 to 8 friends on a WhatsApp call, about 20 minutes each. No code is needed.

## You need

- One **dealer**. They sit out the first game and run it. Everyone takes a turn as dealer later.
- A case from the game: any Rush or Race case. Open it on the dealer's phone and screenshot the setup, the 3 statements and the facts.
- The WhatsApp call, plus a private chat between the dealer and each player.

## How a round goes (both versions)

1. **Deal (2 min).** The dealer sends every player a private card, one message each. The cards follow the version you're playing, below.
2. **Talk (4 min, the dealer keeps time).** Going round the call, each player says one thing about the case in their own words: a time, a place, who was where. Nobody reads their card out word for word. After one full round of turns, it's an open discussion.
3. **Vote (30 s).** On the dealer's count of three, everyone sends the dealer one name by private message.
4. **Reveal.** The dealer reads out the votes, then says who the liar was.

## Version A: the liar knows

- Every **detective** gets the full case.
- The **liar** gets the case with one key fact missing (the fact that proves who is lying), plus the line: "You are the liar. Don't get caught."
- The liar has to bluff about the fact they never saw.
- **Points:** the detectives win if the most-voted player is the liar. The liar wins otherwise. The liar also gets a bonus point if they can say who the case's liar was.

## Version B: the liar doesn't know

- Every player gets the full case and the line "You are a detective."
- One player's card has **one fact quietly changed**, for example 9:10 PM instead of 9:40 PM. Nobody knows they're the odd one out, including that player.
- **Points:** everyone whose vote lands on the changed card scores a point. The player with the changed card scores a point for each person who didn't vote for them.
- **Dealer tip:** change a fact that matters, but not the one people will quote first. A change to the most obvious time gets found in 10 seconds.

## House rules for what can go wrong

| What happens | Rule |
|---|---|
| A tied vote | One more minute of talk between the tied players only, then vote again. A second tie means the liar wins (A), or nobody scores (B). |
| The liar is caught within the first minute | Play the round out anyway. Note it on the checklist: it means the card made the liar too easy to spot. |
| Someone won't talk | Every player must say at least one thing per round. Anyone who passes twice gets votes counted against them as a tiebreak. |
| Someone drops off the call | If it's the liar, the round ends and nobody scores. If it's a detective, carry on without their vote. |
| Someone reads their card out word for word | Their vote doesn't count that round. |

## After each version, write down

- [ ] How many seconds until the first real suspicion?
- [ ] Was the right person voted out? Did it feel earned or lucky?
- [ ] Did every player talk, or did 1 or 2 people do all of it?
- [ ] Did anyone laugh, shout or accuse a friend? That's the fun signal.
- [ ] Did people want another round straight away?
- [ ] Which version would you play again next week, and why?

Play at least 3 rounds of each version with the same group, then bring the notes back. The version that wins becomes the one we build.
