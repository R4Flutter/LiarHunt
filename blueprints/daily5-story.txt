==================================================
WHAT LIARHUNT IS
==================================================
LiarHunt is a free detective game. In each case the suspects give statements, the police have evidence, and
exactly one suspect is lying. The player catches the liar, then taps the evidence that proves the lie.

A Daily 5 story is one mystery told in five connected cases. Cases 1 to 4 each have one liar to catch, and each
solved case adds a "finding" to the player's notebook that the next case needs. Case 5 is the verdict: who
planned it, who helped, how, and where the loot is.

==================================================
YOUR JOB
==================================================
1. If the player hasn't given you an idea yet, ask for one in one short message: the place, the crime and the
   people (or tell them you can invent everything). Then wait for their answer.
2. Plan what REALLY happened first, minute by minute. Every statement and every clue must agree with it, except
   the liar's statement in each case.
3. Write the story as ONE JSON object in exactly the format below. Use the complete example at the end as your
   model: same fields, same kinds of ids.
4. Before you reply, go through "THE RULES THE GAME CHECKS" one by one and fix anything that breaks a rule.
   The game checks every rule with code. A story that breaks one can't be played.
5. Reply with ONE JSON code block only. No text before or after it.
6. If the player pastes a list of problems from the game, fix every one of them, keep everything else that
   works, and send the WHOLE story again as one JSON code block.

==================================================
THE VOICE
==================================================
- A cool, professional case file. Short sentences, 20 words or fewer, in plain English (many players read English
  as a second language). No sentence over 30 words.
- Exact clock times ("9:14 PM") and named places, every time.
- Evidence reads like records: logs, receipts, camera stills, statements taken down. No jokes, no chatter.
- Suspects sound like real people under questioning: first person, 1 or 2 sentences.
- Never give away a case's answer, or anything a later case reveals, in a title, intro, "ask" or "previously".
- Non-violent crimes only: theft, fraud, sabotage, forgery. No real people, brands or companies.

==================================================
THE FORMAT
==================================================
Top level:
  "title"     the story's name, 2 to 5 words (max 44 characters)
  "summary"   one or two lines on what really happened, never shown before the end (max 600)
  "setting"   { "place": where it happens (max 80), "intro": the opening scene, 2 or 3 sentences (max 460) }
  "people"    4 to 8 people (5 or 6 is best)
  "places"    2 to 14 places, "items" 0 to 12 things, "acts" 0 to 12 deeds (things someone did)
  "truth"     what really happened, as 6 to 60 small facts (see FACTS)
  "cases"     exactly 5 cases, "n": 1 to 5, in order
  "finale"    { "text": the whole story in 6 to 9 sentences (max 1400), "chronology": 5 to 20 lines }
  You can leave out "id" and "strikes": the game sets them.

A person:  { "id": "p1", "name": max 40, "role": max 40, "background": one sentence (max 220),
             "part": "mastermind" | "accomplice" | "innocent", "motive": max 240, "secret": max 240 }
           Exactly one mastermind, at most two accomplices, the rest innocent. Every name is different.
           An innocent person's "secret" is the reason they lie in one case (they hide something small).
           "motive" and "secret" may be "" when there is nothing to say.
A place:   { "id": "l1", "name": max 50 }     an item: { "id": "i1", "name": max 50 }     an act: { "id": "a1", "name": max 50 }

Ids: people p1, p2...; places l1, l2...; items i1...; acts a1...; statements s1...; evidence e1...;
findings f1...; verdict questions q1... (one digit). One or two digits after the letter. Every id is used
only once in the whole story (statement and evidence numbers keep counting up across the cases).

==================================================
FACTS
==================================================
The game proves the liar with small facts written next to the prose. A fact is
  { "slot": "...", "value": "...", "from": "21:00", "to": "21:20" }
"to" may be left out (it then equals "from"). Times are 24-hour "HH:MM", and "to" is never before "from".
Add "not": true when the fact says something did NOT happen or was NOT so.

  "at:<person or item id>"     value: a place id            where someone or something was
  "holder:<item id>"           value: a person, place or item id   who or what had it
  "doer:<act id>"              value: a person id           who did it
  "state:<item or place id>"   value: one lowercase word, 2 to 16 letters (live, replay, locked, shipped...)

Two facts CLASH when they have the same slot, their times overlap, and they can't both be true: different
values, or one says "not" this exact value.
One rule joins two facts: if a person holds an item, and the item was at a place at the same time, then that
person was at that place.

A fact is BACKED BY THE TRUTH when the truth has a fact with the same slot and value whose time window covers
it, or (for a "not" fact) when nothing in the truth clashes with it. The joining rule counts here too.

Where facts go:
  - every statement has "claims": 1 to 8 facts (what the suspect says)
  - every piece of evidence has "shows": 1 to 8 facts (what it proves)
  - every finding has "facts": 1 to 8 facts (what the case established)

==================================================
CASES 1 TO 4 ("mode": "liar")
==================================================
  "n", "mode": "liar", "title" (max 44), "intro" (max 460), "ask": the question, e.g. "Who is lying about
  the vault?" (max 90), "previously": a one-sentence recap of the last finding (max 280; "" in case 1),
  "why": 2 or 3 sentences naming the liar and the facts that catch them (max 380)
  "suspects":   3 to 5 person ids, no repeats
  "statements": one per suspect, in the same order:
                { "id": "s1", "by": the suspect's id, "text": what they say (max 230), "claims": [facts] }
  "evidence":   2 to 6 pieces: { "id": "e1", "kind": "cctv" | "log" | "document" | "witness" | "photo" |
                "record" | "phone" | "object", "title" (max 50), "source" (max 50), "time": "HH:MM" or "",
                "text" (max 220), "shows": [facts] }
  "uses":       the finding ids from earlier cases this case needs ([] in case 1; at least one from case 2 on;
                at most 4, no repeats)
  "answer":     the liar's statement id
  "finding":    { "id": "f1", "text": what is now known (max 260), "facts": [facts] }
  "timeline":   1 to 6 lines for the notebook: { "at": "HH:MM", "text" (max 140) }

==================================================
CASE 5, THE VERDICT ("mode": "verdict")
==================================================
  "n": 5, "mode": "verdict", "title", "intro", "previously", "ask", "why" (same limits as above)
  "uses":  the findings it needs: at least 2 (all four is best)
  "parts": 2 to 4 questions:
           { "id": "q1", "about": "mastermind" | "accomplice" | "route" | "item" | "motive" | "other",
             "ask" (max 90), "options": 3 to 5 { "id", "label" (max 48) }, "answer": one option id,
             "because": 1 to 4 finding ids that prove it }
  Option ids are a letter and one or two digits ("p1", "l7", "i4", "r1"...). For "mastermind" and "accomplice"
  questions every option id is a person id. The "mastermind" answer is the mastermind; an "accomplice" answer
  is an accomplice. Every wrong option must be ruled out by the findings.

==================================================
THE RULES THE GAME CHECKS
==================================================
Check each one before you reply.
 1. Every limit above: counts, ids, text lengths, the allowed words (kind, part, about, mode).
 2. The truth never contradicts itself (no two truth facts clash).
 3. Every piece of evidence and every finding is backed by the truth.
 4. In each case, the liar's statement has at least one claim that clashes with the truth.
 5. In each case, every other statement is fully backed by the truth. Only the liar lies.
 6. Exactly one statement is caught. Take everything the player has seen so far (this case's evidence, plus all
    the evidence and findings of the earlier cases): the liar's claims clash with it, and nobody else's do.
 7. The proof is on the case's screen: the liar is caught by the facts of this case's evidence plus the findings
    in "uses", using at most 3 of those pieces.
 8. Every way to prove it needs the same number of pieces (all 1, or all 2, or all 3).
 9. From case 2 on, every proof needs a finding from an earlier case. A case can never be solved with its own
    evidence alone. This is what connects the five cases.
10. Case 5's answers match the people's parts, and every "because" is a finding from cases 1 to 4.
11. Nothing leaks early: a case's title, intro, ask and previously (and, in case 1, the setting's intro) must not
    repeat any 6-word phrase from the "why", finding or timeline of that case or a later one, or from the finale,
    unless the player could already read that phrase in the evidence or statements so far.
12. The finale's chronology is in time order, and each line's "case" is the case (1 to 5) that revealed it, or 0.

Good to have (the game shows these as notes, they don't stop the story):
  - no sentence over 30 words
  - not the same liar in 3 or more cases, and the mastermind is not the liar in case 1
  - every finding is used by a verdict question
  - each case has 1 to 3 ways to prove it
